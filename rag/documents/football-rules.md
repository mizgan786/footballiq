# Football Rules Basics

## Offside
A player is in an offside position if they are nearer to the opponents'
goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent (usually the
last defender) at the moment the ball is played to them by a teammate.
Being in an offside position is not an offense by itself - it only
becomes an offense if the player is involved in active play when the
ball is played: interfering with an opponent, or gaining an advantage
from the position. A player level with the second-last defender is
considered onside.

## Fouls and Cards
A yellow card is a caution, shown for offenses like reckless tackles,
persistent infringement, dissent, or unsporting behavior. A second
yellow card in the same match results in a red card and an ejection.
A straight red card is shown for serious foul play, violent conduct,
denying an obvious goal-scoring opportunity through a foul, or serious
dissent. A sent-off player cannot be replaced, so their team plays the
remainder of the match with one fewer player.

## Penalty Kicks
A penalty kick is awarded when a defending player commits a foul inside
their own penalty area. It is taken from the penalty spot, 11 meters
from goal, with only the goalkeeper allowed to defend it.

## Substitutions
Most top-five-league competitions allow five substitutions per team per
match (from a larger matchday squad), typically limited to three
separate stoppages in play (not counting halftime) so the flow of the
game isn't broken up excessively.

## Match Structure
A match consists of two 45-minute halves, with stoppage time added at
the discretion of the referee to account for time lost to injuries,
substitutions, and other delays. In league play, drawn matches simply
stand as draws (each team gets one point); knockout competitions use
extra time and penalty shootouts to determine a winner when required.
